Background diagnostic

A 4-column quality-control figure per channel that lets you see — at a glance — whether the polynomial background fit is doing its job. Look at this before trusting any other figure.

How to read this figure

  1. Column 1: are the sample dots on cells? (bad)
  2. Column 2: is the surface smooth? (good)
  3. Column 3: are cells visible as dark spots? (good)
  4. Column 4: does background drift wildly? (concern)
Purpose

The "did the bg model work" sanity check

The single most consequential preprocessing step is background subtraction (→ details). If the polynomial model captures vignetting/drift well, every downstream figure is on solid ground. If it doesn't — sample points landed on cells, the surface has artifacts, the trace drifts unaccountably — every downstream result is suspect. This figure gives you the four lenses you need to spot trouble.

Inputs

What it reads from state

  • state["probe_data"][exp][ch] — the middle frame (raw + corrected) plus annotations (frame index, mean intensity at sample points, fitted bg min/max).
  • state["bg_map_by_ch"][exp][ch] — the 2D fitted background surface evaluated on a fine grid at the probe frame.
  • state["bg_trace"][exp][ch] — for every frame, mean intensity at all sample points. (n_frames,) array.
  • state["bg_sample_points_exp"][exp] — the (x, y) coordinates of the cell-free sample points for that experiment.
Method

What the script does

  1. For each experiment, create one figure: 4 columns × N channels rows.
  2. For each channel, render the four panels (see below).
  3. Title each panel with summary stats (frame index, sample mean, bg min/max).
  4. Save as {exp_name}_bg_diagnostic.png at 100 dpi.

Figure size: 22 inches wide × 5 inches per channel-row.

Col 1 · Probe + sample points

The middle frame as grayscale (0–255), with the cell-free sample points overlaid as small colored dots (α=0.6). Annotated with frame index and mean sample intensity. Look for: sample dots not on cells. If they overlap visible features, the fit will be biased.

Col 2 · Fitted background

The 2D polynomial surface as a heatmap, with colorbar and min/max annotation. Look for: smooth gradient (e.g. center-to-edge vignetting). Sharp local features here mean the polynomial is overfit or the sample points are anomalous.

Col 3 · Corrected probe

probe − fitted_bg on the same 0–255 scale as col 1. Look for: cells now appear as dim/dark patches against a flat near-zero background. If background still has structure, the polynomial wasn't enough. If cells are still bright, the dynamic range was clipped at 0 by overcorrection.

Col 4 · Sampled bg over time

Mean sample-point intensity per frame, x-axis in minutes (via frames_to_min). Vertical dashed line marks the probe frame. Look for: smooth drift (acceptable, the polynomial is per-frame so it tracks), abrupt jumps (focus shift / stage move), or noisy spikes (dropout candidates for the dead-frame mask).

Outputs

Files produced

April28_preprint_results/<exp_name>/{exp_name}_bg_diagnostic.png

One PNG per experiment, regardless of channel count. With NRK (4 channels) the figure is 4 rows tall.

What the cell biology says

Expected trends · interpretation

Healthy fit

Col 2 is a smooth bowl. Col 3 has near-uniform dark background with cells barely visible (correction has done its job). Col 4 is a slow monotonic decline (luminescence decay) or near-flat trace.

Sample-point contamination

Col 2 has bumps. Col 3 has bright artifacts where sample points landed on cells. Col 4 is unusually high. The fit absorbed cell signal.

Real biological drift

Col 4 trends down monotonically — that's the bioluminescent reporter consuming substrate. This is expected and the polynomial corrects for it. Worry only when the trace is non-monotonic with sharp features.

Hardware wobble

Col 4 has step changes corresponding to stage movements / focus drift / illumination changes. The per-frame polynomial absorbs the smooth components; sharp steps may leave residual drift in corrected_lum.

Dead-frame candidates

Col 4 has occasional sharp spikes (camera dropouts). For PC3 these are masked by the dead-frame filter; for C2C12 / NRK, they pass through and may show up in time-trace pages as outlier frames.

Channel imbalance

Different channels have different sample-point counts and bg dynamics. Compare panels across channel-rows to confirm the sampler chose enough points everywhere.

Watch out

Pitfalls

Common ways this can mislead you

Probe is one frame. Cols 1–3 show the middle frame only. Cells that drift, appear, or disappear later are invisible here. The 15-mask snapshots used for sample-point exclusion try to compensate, but a cell that moves into a sample point after all snapshots is invisible to this check.

Color-scale alignment. Cols 1 and 3 use a fixed 0–255 scale. If the corrected probe has values clipped at 0 because of overcorrection, that information is suppressed. Look at col 4 and at downstream time-trace plots for confirmation.

Spatial features ≠ temporal stability. Even if cols 1–3 look great, col 4 with a wobbly trace tells you the model is not capturing temporal variation between sample-point measurements. Per-frame fitting helps but isn't a free lunch.

It's a per-experiment figure. No cross-experiment comparison. To compare bg quality across experiments, look at multiple PNGs side-by-side.
