dF/F0 normalization

The same traces as the time-traces page, but expressed as fractional change from each cell's own baseline. Removes brightness differences between cells and makes responses comparable. The basis for almost every downstream analysis.

How to read this figure

  1. Bottom-panel zero-crossings = baseline.
  2. Bold mean traces tell the population story.
  3. Tan-shaded F0 window = how F0 was estimated.
  4. Histogram = response-magnitude distribution (C2C12 only).
Purpose

Make cells comparable

Two cells can have wildly different absolute brightness (more reporter, brighter pixel, etc.) yet respond similarly in relative terms. dF/F0 = (F − F0)/F0 normalizes each cell to its own pre-stim baseline so a 30% rise looks like a 30% rise everywhere. Without this normalization, the population mean is dominated by the brightest cells. With it, every cell contributes equally to the population picture.

Inputs

What it reads from state

  • state["corrected_lum"][exp][ch] — the bg-corrected per-cell traces (after pipeline).
  • cfg["stim_frames"][ch] — used to locate the F0 window ([0, first_stim)) and to build response-window peaks.
  • cfg["response_direction"] — "increase" or "decrease".
  • cfg["response_window"] — frame offsets (lo, hi) after each stim.
  • Responder thresholds — computed by responders.py and used for the responder-pooled-mean panel.
Method

The dF/F0 calculation

# For every cell:
F0 = mean(corrected_lum[cell, frames 0..first_stim])     # nan if no valid frames
F0 = nan if F0 == 0                                       # avoid /0

dff = (corrected_lum[cell, :] − F0) / F0                  # per-frame fractional change

# Per-cell peak per stim:
for stim in stim_frames:
    window = dff[cell, stim+lo : stim+hi]
    peak[cell, stim] = max(window) − dff[cell, stim]      # for "increase"
                       min(window) − dff[cell, stim]      # for "decrease"

# Per-cell overall peak:
overall_peak[cell] = max over stims of |peak[cell, stim]|

For the response-magnitude histogram, the script ignores stim ordering and pools every (cell × stim) peak into one distribution.

What the cell biology says

Expected trends · interpretation

Healthy DMSO response

Top panel rises sharply at each stim, partial recovery between stims, slow drift over the experiment. Mean dF/F0 has visible bumps at every shaded region. Histogram has a population at |dF/F0| ≥ 0.05 with a tail toward 0.5+.

Healthy NRK acid response

Mean dF/F0 dips below 0 at each stim, then partially recovers. Distribution skewed toward negative, captured as |dF/F0| via responder threshold. The first 30 min show the controller-stabilization regime.

Population habituation

Across the experiment, mean dF/F0 peaks shrink with successive stims. Visible directly in the bottom panel of {ch}_dff.png. Quantified more rigorously in learning_scores.

Population sensitization

Mean dF/F0 peaks grow with successive stims. Often noisier than habituation because amplification is rare in mature cells.

F0 window choice working

The tan F0 shading (frames 0..first_stim) sits over a flat region. Baseline jitter is low. F0 is a stable estimate. dF/F0 traces start near 0 in the F0 window.

Bimodal histogram

In the response-breakdown panel, a clear gap between a peak near 0 (non-responders) and a peak at higher |dF/F0| (responders) suggests two distinct populations. A single broad peak suggests a continuum.

Watch out

Pitfalls

Where this can go subtly wrong

F0 contamination. If anything happened in the F0 window (early stim, bg artifact, calibration flash), F0 is biased and every dF/F0 value is off by the same multiplicative factor. The tan-shaded F0 window in {ch}_dff.png is your visual check.

F0 = 0 or near-zero. A cell with very dim baseline produces NaN or wildly inflated dF/F0. The script sets F0=0 cases to NaN, but cells with F0 ≈ small positive numbers can still produce unrealistically large dF/F0. Watch for cell traces at the top edge of the y-axis in the bottom panel.

Threshold sensitivity. The response-breakdown histogram includes 0.02 / 0.05 / 0.10 as visual references. Reported responder fractions can shift dramatically across these. Reviewer context matters; the Bonferroni threshold from responders.py is more rigorous but isn't shown on this figure.

Cross-channel pooling requires alignment. The responder-pooled mean is only generated when stim timing is the same across channels. NRK channels with feedback control rarely satisfy this.

Mean masks heterogeneity. The bold black mean tells you the population direction, not the spread. A small mean response could be two strongly responding sub-populations canceling out. Pair this figure with clustering and response violins.
