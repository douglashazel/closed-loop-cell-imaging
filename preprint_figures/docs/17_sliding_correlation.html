Sliding correlation

Filter to non-trivially-correlated pairs, then walk a window across the experiment computing Pearson and Spearman in real-time. Reveals when the population synchronizes (rises) or desynchronizes (falls). Slow; off in run_figures.sh by default.

How to read this figure

  1. Bold mean line is the population-average pair correlation.
  2. Faint background lines are individual pairs.
  3. Vertical lines mark stims — synchronization often peaks here.
  4. Pearson and Spearman should agree if linear; divergence = nonlinearity.
Purpose

How does synchronization evolve over time?

Two cells responding to the same stimulus tend to be correlated during the response and uncorrelated outside it. By computing pairwise correlation in a sliding window, we can watch synchronization rise and fall across the experiment. Peaks at stims = stim-driven sync. Persistent positive correlation between stims = ongoing coupling. A drift toward zero = the population is becoming independent.

Computational note: this is the slowest analysis in the pipeline. The orchestrator run_figures.sh excludes it from "all" by default; enable explicitly with ANALYSES="sliding_correlation ...".

Inputs

What it reads from state

  • state["corrected_lum"][exp][ch] — bg-corrected per-cell traces.
  • cfg["stim_frames"][ch] — for the stim onset markers in the plot.
  • state["real_setpoint_min"][exp][ch] — for the real setpoint marker (NRK only).
  • Plot params: PLOT_PARAMS_SLIDING sets window size (~30 frames), step (~15 frames), SEM multiplier.
Method

Pre-filter, then slide

  1. Compute full-experiment Pearson per pair. All N(N−1)/2 pairs. This is once-off.
  2. Filter pairs. Keep pairs with full-experiment r < cutoff where cutoff = pp["global_corr_cutoff"]. The default focuses on not-strongly-correlated pairs — the rationale being that pairs with already-high global r are uninteresting (always correlated), and we want to see whether previously uncorrelated pairs become correlated during stims.
  3. Sliding window. For each window center t ∈ [half_w, n_frames − half_w) stepped by step:
    • Extract trace segment [t − half_w, t + half_w).
    • For each filtered pair: compute Pearson + Spearman on the segment. Skip if std < 1e-8 (zero-variance — flat segment).
  4. Aggregate. Per window: mean, std, SEM, n_valid pairs.
  5. Plot. Two stacked panels (Pearson top, Spearman bottom) with: faint individual-pair lines, bold mean, ±n×SEM shaded envelope (n from sem_n, default 2), vertical stim onset lines, vertical dotted setpoint marker.

The work scales as O(n_pairs_filtered × n_windows). With ~200 cells (20k pairs) and ~500 windows, that's 10M correlation computations per channel. tqdm progress bar tracks the work.

What the cell biology says

Expected trends · interpretation

Synchronizing population

Mean correlation rises during stim windows, drops after. Cells respond together → r increases temporarily. The amplitude of the rise indicates how strong the synchronization is. A common stim-driven outcome.

Persistent coupling

Mean correlation stays high even between stims. Could indicate stable cell-cell signaling (gap junctions, paracrine), or that the stims have left a lasting coordinated state.

No coordination

Mean correlation hovers near 0 throughout. Cells respond independently. The pre-filter removed strong pairs, so this is the expected baseline; deviation from 0 around stims is the signal.

Pearson ≠ Spearman

Pearson detects linear relationships; Spearman detects monotonic ones. If Pearson and Spearman traces diverge, the underlying relationship is nonlinear. Common when one cell saturates while another grows linearly.

Wide SEM envelope

The mean is uncertain — many pairs disagree. Could mean some pairs synchronize while others anti-correlate. The faint background lines should show this directly.

Real setpoint discontinuity (NRK)

Watch for a step in the mean correlation at the dotted setpoint line. The hardware switched modes; population dynamics may shift.

Watch out

Pitfalls

The biggest issue: the pre-filter

Cutoff choice frames the question. global_corr_cutoff determines which pairs are tracked. Too high → most pairs included → mean correlation is dominated by already-correlated pairs and barely changes over time. Too low → only weakly-correlated pairs included → mean is noisy because filtered pairs are already near zero. The default value lives in plot_params.py.

Computational cost. Hundreds of thousands of correlation computations per channel. For a 200-cell experiment with 1000+ frames, expect minutes per channel. The script is opt-in for this reason.

Window size trade-off. Large windows smooth noise but blur fast events (you can't see a 30 s stim response in a 30-frame window if the frame rate is 1 Hz — that's the whole window). Small windows capture sharp events but produce noisy correlation estimates from limited data points.

Successive windows are not independent. Overlapping windows produce smoothed-but-correlated samples. Apparent "events" in the trace might just be the smoothing kernel responding to a single underlying spike. Treat the trace as a smooth visualization, not as N independent measurements.

NaN handling is silent. Pairs with NaN in a window are skipped (continue), reducing n_valid for that window. The mean still plots, but is over fewer pairs and may be less reliable.

Spearman with ties. Cells saturated at the detector ceiling have many tied values; Spearman behaves unpredictably with heavy ties. If your traces hit max counts, prefer Pearson.

Frame-rate assumption. The window size is in frames, but the x-axis is in minutes. Variable frame rates would distort the time interpretation of window size. Check the time axis if frame rate is uneven.
