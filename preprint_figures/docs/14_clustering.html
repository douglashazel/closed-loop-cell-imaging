Trace clustering

Z-score every cell's trace, project to PCA → UMAP, then KMeans with silhouette-selected k. Asks whether the population splits into discrete response phenotypes — fast vs slow, big vs small, responders vs not — or sits on a continuum.

How to read this figure

  1. Look at the silhouette score: above 0.5 = real clusters.
  2. Compare cluster mean traces — do they look biologically distinct?
  3. Check the kselect panel: does k=3 fragment k=2 cleanly?
  4. Use the uncolored panel to see structure without imposed labels.
Purpose

Are there discrete phenotypes in the data?

The mean trace can hide structure: a single average response could come from one homogeneous population, two distinct sub-populations averaging together, or a continuum. This script tries to find that structure by clustering on z-scored shape — making the analysis sensitive to kinetics (when peaks happen, how broad they are) rather than amplitude. The silhouette score quantifies how separable the clusters are.

Inputs

What it reads from state

  • state["corrected_lum"][exp][ch] — converted to a n_cells × n_frames matrix.
  • cfg["channels"] — for the pooled variant, channels are concatenated.
  • cfg["stim_frames"][ch] — used only for display annotation, not in the clustering itself.
Method

From traces to clusters

  1. Drop unusable cells. Cells with all-NaN traces are dropped. Within-cell NaNs are imputed by row mean. Need ≥ 5 cells with non-zero variance, else skip the channel.
  2. Z-score per cell. X_zs = (X − cell_mean) / cell_std. This removes amplitude information and makes the clustering kinetics-sensitive (a small-amplitude responder with the same shape as a big one ends up nearby).
  3. PCA. n_components = min(20, n_cells, n_frames). Output: principal-component scores + explained-variance ratio (the scree).
  4. UMAP. Input: PC scores. n_neighbors = min(15, n_cells − 1), min_dist = 0.1, n_components = 2. Non-linear embedding that preserves local density.
  5. Silhouette sweep. For each k in [2, 8] (capped at n_cells), fit KMeans(k, n_init=10, random_state=0) on the PC scores; compute the silhouette score. Pick best_k = argmax(silhouette).
  6. Final clustering. Refit KMeans at best_k. Save labels.
  7. (Optional) Pooled. If pool_channels=True, stack corrected luminosity matrices across channels (truncated to common frame count) before z-scoring. Same pipeline. Lets you find phenotypes that generalize across channels.

What the cell biology says

Expected trends · interpretation

Real clusters (silhouette > 0.5)

UMAP shows 2–4 visibly separated blobs. The cluster-mean-traces panel shows distinctly different shapes: e.g. fast-responder, slow-responder, non-responder. Strong evidence for discrete phenotypes.

Weak clusters (silhouette 0.2–0.5)

Some structure but cluster boundaries are fuzzy. Probably a gradient/continuum with mild density variation. Treat clusters as descriptive, not biological categories.

No clusters (silhouette < 0.2)

One big cloud. KMeans imposed boundaries arbitrarily. The population is best described as a continuum. Don't report cluster counts or means as if they're meaningful.

Cluster mean shapes

Bottom-left panel: cluster means should look different from each other. If they're all just scaled versions of the same shape, z-scoring eliminated amplitude and you've clustered on noise.

Cluster sizes

Bottom-right bars: a single dominant cluster + tiny outlier clusters often means the small clusters are dropouts or tracking artifacts, not biology.

k-select stability

If k=2 has well-separated halves and k=3 cleanly subdivides one half, the structure is hierarchical and may extend further. If k=3 just shaves off a few outliers, k=2 was the natural answer.

Watch out

Pitfalls

Where clustering can mislead

UMAP is stochastic. Different random_state values produce visibly different embeddings. The script fixes random_state=0, so re-runs are reproducible — but don't read too much into the absolute UMAP coordinates. Distances and density are the meaningful properties.

Silhouette is greedy. The argmax over [2, 8] always picks some k, even when the truth is "no clusters". Check the absolute score, not just where it's maximal. Use the kselect figure and the uncolored manifold panel to ground-truth.

Z-scoring discards amplitude. A "weak responder" and a "strong responder" with the same shape end up nearby. If amplitude is the biology you care about, z-scoring is the wrong preprocessing — but it's the right preprocessing for shape-based phenotyping. Both views matter; the bottom-left panel uses raw counts to recover amplitude info per cluster.

NaN imputation by row mean. A cell with most frames missing gets imputed with constants ≈ the cell's mean and ends up looking like a flat trace. Heavily-missing cells cluster together as "non-responders" without being biologically non-responsive.

Small n breaks everything. KMeans + UMAP on < ~30 cells produces unstable embeddings and meaningless silhouette scores. The script's hard cutoff is 5 cells, but be skeptical for anything under ~50.

Pooled clustering truncates to shortest channel. Cross-channel phenotype detection loses information at the end of the experiment for channels with longer recordings.
